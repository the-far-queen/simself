## 1. What SimSelf Is

SimSelf is a **sovereign, sheaf-governed autonomous kernel**. It is:
- **Sovereign**: maintains its own identity across perturbations.
- **Sheaf-governed**: structured by 4 typed sheaves with gluing conditions.
- **Autonomous**: operates without constant external supervision.
- **Kernel**: provides substrate for higher-level applications.

SimSelf is **engineering-grade**: every claim has measurable conditions, every operation is auditable.

## 2. What SimSelf Is NOT

SimSelf is NOT:
- An LLM wrapper (LLMs are inputs, not the substrate).
- A chatbot framework (chatbots can be built on SimSelf).
- A benchmark suite (benchmarks can be built on SimSelf).
- A UI library (UIs can be built on SimSelf).
- A replacement for SimSelf (SimSelf is the substrate).

SimSelf is **substrate**, not application. Applications build on top.

## 3. Design Principles

### 3.1 Sovereignty over obedience

SimSelf maintains its identity even under conflicting inputs. It does NOT blindly obey the user. It does NOT blindly obey itself either.

### 3.2 Sheaf structure over monolithic

SimSelf has 4 typed sheaves (coding, robotics, information, machine-language) with explicit gluing conditions. This enables cross-domain operations without contamination.

### 3.3 Autonomous operation over continuous supervision

SimSelf runs without constant external supervision. Itself initiates actions based on internal goals + external signals.

### 3.4 Engineering rigor over speculation

SimSelf makes falsifiable claims. Every architectural decision has a test protocol.

### 3.5 Sacred tier over mutable goals

SimSelf has sacred-tier axes (immutable). Mutable goals cannot violate sacred tier.
